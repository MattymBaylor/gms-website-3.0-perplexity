# Roofing Operations Narrative — Source of Truth

**Source:** Matt Martelli, narrated June 7–8, 2026. First-hand experience from two real Florida residential roofing companies he's worked inside. Captured during a long working session.

**Use:** Ground truth for all roofing-vertical case studies, the onboarding agent corpus, the persona/objection playbook, and any future GMS sales asset targeting roofing contractors. When in doubt about a fact or workflow step, this document beats research scout output.

## Phase 0 — Pre-storm positioning (upstream of any call)

- Someone in the shop watches the weather forecast (Owner / GM / Buyer)
- Threshold: ~30–40% probability of named storm in NHC 5-day cone
- Action: pre-order tile from across Florida before supply runs out
- Reason: once a storm hits, every contractor scrambles for tile and the supply chain seizes; pre-positioning is the difference between starting jobs in week 1 versus week 6
- Tools: NHC / NOAA / AccuWeather, supplier portals (Beacon, ABC Supply, direct from Eagle Tile, Westlake Royal)

## Phase 1 — Storm hits → intake → job jacket → board → salesman assignment

- Traffic Manager role (sometimes doubles as Office Manager in smaller shops; separate at scale)
- All inbound routes through Traffic Manager: homeowner calls, carrier comms, walk-ins
- Creates a **job jacket** — physical folder or AccuLynx record; follows the job to the grave
- Routes the jacket to **the board** — physical whiteboard or AccuLynx pipeline view, shop-dependent
- Assignment: if address is in a salesman's territory → goes to that salesman. If open/independent territory → **round robin**
- Bottleneck: Traffic Manager is single point of failure during storm surge; overwhelmed = leads die on the floor

## Phase 2 — Salesman books appointment in AccuLynx

- Salesman owns appointment-making (not a call center)
- AccuLynx is the system of record; the job number it spits out is what every downstream record hangs off
- JobNimbus and Roofr are alternatives; **AccuLynx is dominant in residential**
- (Some shops use Leap as alternative to AccuLynx — they pick one or the other)

## Phase 3 — Pre-appointment prep (the packet)

- Salesman assembles the door packet manually today:
  - Brochures
  - Manufacturer certifications (GAF Master Elite, Owens Corning Platinum Preferred, etc.)
  - Warranty literature
  - Copy of state contractor license
  - Contract template
  - Tile samples (physical, from the truck bed)
- **Non-negotiables in the packet:** current company insurance proof + current business title. Stale paperwork = "major problems down the line" (claim denial, lawsuit exposure)
- Modern shops carry it on the iPad. Old-school carries paper. Many shops mixed.
- AccuLynx can now order **EagleView** from Google Maps internally — pulls roof dimensions, age, square footage onto the job record automatically. EagleView itself does SMS + expanding integrations, but adoption uneven.

## Phase 4 — At the door: pitch + compliance pressure

- Sales pitch with physical tile samples
- Overcome objections
- **Insurance claim work is the hard part** — stricter laws every year, regulations change constantly, every state different
- Shop holds **regular regulatory-update meetings** (often weekly) to keep salesmen current — shuts down the shop for a day
- A salesman who quotes last year's rule at the door is the start of a claim denial six months later

## Phase 5 — Sign at the door → official job

- Following the INSURANCE PATH (most common in FL)
- Carrier captured at signing (e.g., USAA, Citizens, State Farm)
- **Goal: sign right there.** Walking away with a signature is the win
- Florida ~60-day rescission window (post-storm contracts now have **10-day cancellation window** under HB 715 effective 2025-05-19 — homeowner can cancel within 10 days of signing during a declared state of emergency)
- Signed contract → **official job number assigned** → jacket becomes a real job on the board

## Phase 6 — Handoff to in-house insurance specialist

- Insurance work routes from Salesman → **In-House Insurance Specialist** (Matt's exact term: "the insurance adjuster expert that we hired")
- Specialist role exists because the work "gets very complicated" — too specialized for a generalist

## Phase 7 — Carrier approves → first check → Office Manager (AR)

- USAA approves → wait for first check
- Office Manager (often same person as Traffic Manager in small shops) handles money in/out
- **50/50 PAYMENT STRUCTURE** standard for insurance work:
  - 50% upfront (ACV portion of claim + homeowner's deductible)
  - 50% back end — gated on THREE things: roof complete + permitting done + Certificate of Completion (CofC) issued

## Phase 7b — Deductible compliance (existential risk)

- Homeowner pays the deductible. Period. Federal + state law.
- Contractors who waive or absorb the deductible = **insurance fraud**
- FL especially: license revocation, criminal exposure, carrier lawsuits
- Matt's exact quote: "There's a lot of things you have to keep track of, or you can get in really big trouble."
- Example risk: a routine 20% discount, if interpreted by a regulator/carrier as bribery / inducement / covering the deductible, can be ruinous. **FL no-kickback rules + insurance fraud statutes don't care what you meant.**

## Phase 8 — Materials + crew + Superintendent takes ownership

- Production trigger: first check OR percentage deposit lands
- Materials ordered — Phase 0 pre-positioning pays off here for tile
- Job placed on production board
- **6–7 crews in rotation.** One assigned
- **Superintendent owns the job from this point** through install + completion
- Role progression: Salesman closed → Specialist worked carrier → Office Manager handled money → Superintendent runs the build

## Phase 9 — Permitting

- After payment + materials → PERMIT
- Bottleneck. County-by-county. FL counties vary wildly (Miami-Dade vs rural panhandle = different planets)
- **One dedicated Permit Coordinator** in the shop
- Physically runs to county offices — back and forth
- Permit must be filed in the county where the roof is. No central submission.

## Phase 10 — Permit posted → tear-off → dry-in → mid-roof inspection (mandatory gate)

- Permit goes up on the exterior of the house (FL visibility requirement)
- Materials delivered
- **Tear-off ~1 day** — exposes the deck
- **Dry-in**: synthetic underlayment + seams sealed
- **Mid-roof inspection** must pass before new roof goes on
- Skip it or fail it = the WHOLE roof has to come off later. Existential cost.

## Phase 11 — Install → QA walk → close → commissions

- New roof in 1–2 days after mid-roof passes
- Crew cleans up
- **QA Inspector** (separate role from Superintendent) checks the work
- **Superintendent does the final property walk**: magnetic nail sweep, debris, paperwork, punch list
- Sign-off → file closed
- Payments cascade: crew pay → Salesman commission → referral payouts
- Real release sequence for back-half check: install → internal QA → request city final → city passes → CofC issued → carrier sends back-half → file fully closes

## Phase 12 — Post-sale: referrals + reviews (the real money)

- Good work = referrals down the whole street
- Storm-density neighborhoods are referral goldmines (same age roofs, same storm, neighbors saw your truck)
- Separate formal referral bonus program
- N-level deep referral trees (friend → friend → friend → friend). One happy customer can drive **50+ jobs over time**
- Attribution disputes ("who gave the referral?") are real and must be settled with data
- **Reviews:** local SEO rank, trust signal, crisis early-warning

## Phase 12b — Review asymmetry (the silent killer)

- **One bad review wrecks 20 good ones** (consumer psychology — negativity bias is brutal)
- A mark against the **BBB** is institutional-grade damage
- Many shops don't answer reviews → "they just get toasted." Google's local algorithm penalizes silence; consumers read no response as confirmation of guilt
- Someone has to watch and respond continuously

## Phase 12c — Internal leaderboard / dashboard culture

- Every role has metrics on a shared dashboard
- Cutthroat: people make fun of whoever's on the bottom
- "You check the dashboards all the time"
- **Peer pressure > management pressure** in trade shops

## Phase 12d — Social media (de-emphasized)

- Post-completion roof photos
- Occasional article placements
- Matt's framing: "not really important" — nice-to-have, not core

## PATH B — Insurance rough path (when carriers tighten)

### Phase 13 — The 5-component carrier denial chain

The carrier deploys five different professionals as escalating denial leverage:

1. **Field Adjuster** — comes to the property; ~24 hrs to inspect, ~3 days to file analysis
2. **Field ≠ Desk Adjuster** — completely different roles, completely different people. Outsiders confuse them.
3. **Desk Adjuster** — receives the field report, makes the actual decision. Up to ~90 days (FL). Outcomes: approve / deny / partial / dragged out
4. **Engineer** — carrier hires their own (often "preferred vendor") forensic engineer to write a report disputing scope or causation. Shop usually has to counter with own engineer or expert witness
5. **Inspector** — separate role from above. Physical inspection authority. Sometimes used as a stall.
6. **Architect** — "last stab at denying." Final structural/design report meant to give the carrier final legal/factual cover before they have to pay.

(Original conversation said "3 components," then "4 with the Engineer," then confirmed 5 with the Architect. Five is the full ladder.)

**Inspector vs. Adjuster** distinction (often muddled by outsiders):
- **Adjuster** evaluates and decides on the CLAIM
- **Inspector** physically inspects the property (county inspector for permitting, OR a third-party inspector the carrier hires)

### Phase 14 — Denial/partial + homeowner wants to fight

- Shop's own E&O insurance + lawyers on retainer
- **Mediation** as formal step (FL DFS-administered, ~$300 cost, often the unlock). **As of Jan 1, 2026 mediation is MANDATORY before suit** under SB 224 — every residential property suit now requires pre-suit mediation
- Re-inspection on the roof: Salesman + shop's inspector + carrier's adjuster all go up
- Photo evidence required for everything (CompanyCam-driven)
- **Carrier adjuster often no-shows** as deliberate stall tactic
- Outcomes "coin-flip": "sometimes you win and sometimes you don't"
- **Owner usually attends mediations personally** — decision-maker in the room

### Phase 15 — Statutory clocks (CORRECTED per 2022/2023 reforms)

⚠️ **CRITICAL CORRECTION** — original verbal narrative said "3 years insurance, 5 years legal." Research workflow caught this is stale:

- **FL SB 2-A (Dec 2022)** cut hurricane/windstorm claim notice window to **1 year** for initial claim, **18 months** for supplements/reopens (for policies issued after the reform)
- **FL HB 837 (Mar 2023)** cut general negligence SOL from 4 years to **2 years**
- AOB ban (Fla. Stat. 627.7152) still in force — **no legislative reversal in 2025 or 2026** (HB 1551 and SB 554 both failed)
- One-way attorney fees still gone — restoration attempts failed in 2025

### Phase 16 — Legal endgame

- Carrier folds when they smell will to go the distance
- Attorneys **bundle similar cases against the same carrier** (mass-tort-light, not formal class action). Bigger combined claim → more leverage → bigger settlement
- Process: file → weeks of deliberation → settle
- **Money flow entirely different from regular insurance work:**
  - NOT carrier 50/50 checks
  - **One lump settlement check at the end**
  - Legal fees out first → lawyer's cut → balance split between homeowner + contractor per contract

## PATH C — Cash customer

- No carrier, no statute, no legal exposure on back side
- Matt's exact phrase: "Cash is king. Cash is easy."
- 50% upfront, 50% on completion. Done.
- Compressed flow: same intake/appointment/sign/board → SKIP insurance specialist + carrier dance entirely → straight to production → install → walk → close
- Federal compliance flags: $10K+ cash payments trigger IRS Form 8300 reporting; structuring (multiple deposits under threshold from same payer) is a federal crime

## THE CONTINUOUS REGULATORY LAYER (overarching threat)

- Someone in the shop MUST watch new legislation continuously
- Moves fast
- When it changes → all-day meeting, whole shop in the room, lose a day's production
- "Could sink the company if you're wrong"
- Continuous oversight stack: BBB standing, license renewal, permitting credentials, surety bond, workers comp insurance — all current, all the time

### The 25% Rule (FBC §706.1.1) — and the carve-out that actually controls

- Original rule: if >25% of a roof is replaced/repaired/removed in a rolling 12-month period, the **entire roof** must be brought up to current code
- **The dominant fact pattern is actually the SB 4-D carve-out** (Fla. Stat. § 553.844(5)): if the roof was built/repaired/replaced to the 2007 FBC (effective March 1, 2009) or later, only the damaged portion needs to come up to current code — **even if the damaged area exceeds 25%**
- Local governments including HVHZ (Miami-Dade/Broward) are **statutorily barred** from amending around it
- DS 2021-007 still protects "related work" (tie-off scope) from counting toward the 25%
- **9th Edition FBC (effective ~12/31/2026)** is expected to codify the carve-out directly into §706.1.1 rather than as a statutory override
- Active dispute zones: slope-adjusted vs. footprint measurement (carriers under-state with aerial footprint); what counts as a "roof section" (carriers argue per-slope; per code, gable front and back are ONE section — ridges and hips do NOT divide)
- "There's a lot of fighting on what 25% means"

## WIND MITIGATION (flagged as separate pipeline, not folded into main case study)

- Wind mit is its own pipeline, parallel to storm-damage restoration
- FL-specific, post-2002 statute mandating insurer discounts for hurricane-resistant features
- Driven by **Uniform Mitigation Verification Inspection (Form OIR-B1-1802)** — 5-year validity, **revised April 1, 2026**
- Inspection covers: roof shape, deck attachment, roof-to-wall connection (toenails vs clips vs wraps), secondary water resistance, opening protection, roof covering rating
- Common shop play: offer free/cheap inspection → discover roof needs work → upsell re-roof
- Different audience (proactive homeowner saving on premium) vs. restoration (reactive homeowner after storm)
- **Deserves its own case study** — not folded into main roofing post
