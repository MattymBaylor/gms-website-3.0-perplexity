1 · The one rule

Be first, then be persistent.

A homeowner who fills out a form is talking to three or four contractors at once. The first one to reach them wins the appointment about 80% of the time. After that first half-minute, the winner is whoever keeps showing up — seven live attempts in ten days, on more than one channel, then a month of light-touch nurture — without becoming a pest.

30sThe standard for the first human dial on every shared lead. Rise's text and email land in about 6 seconds; the call follows inside 30.
4×Lift in contact rate from calling within the first minute versus the first hour.
80%Of the time, the first contractor to make contact gets the appointment.
50%Of homeowners prefer text or email to a phone call. The text opens the door; the call walks through it.
The three non-negotiables.
① Under 30 seconds on every shared lead. Thirty, never sixty.
② At least six live touch points before any lead leaves the active cadence. Six is the floor, not the retire point — the cadence makes seven dial attempts by Day 10.
③ Leads don't die on the vine. Forty-five days of work, then Rise owns the lead and it changes campaign. Nothing retires.
The contact sequence and the benchmarks on this page come from the Modernize onboarding deck (contact sequence; 30-second, 391%, 80%, 50% and 1 agent = 20 leads / 150 dials benchmarks).

Three habits that make the rule real

  • Dial inside 30 seconds of the alert. The #leads alert and the desk list are the signal; the engine rings the desk the moment a lead lands. Two calls back to back ("double tap"), then move on.
  • Never skip a day of the cadence. The task list decides what gets called today, not memory.
  • Log it or it didn't happen. Set the Status or Desk Category after every dial — that record is how the automatic texts know to stop, and how we win credits from the vendor. Ticking "Called Today" alone does not stop the texts.

3 · The 45-day lifecycle

Ten days of active work, a month of nurture, then Rise owns the lead.

The person owns the calls; the machine owns everything that repeats. Every purchased lead gets 45 days of work: ten days of active cadence, a month of light-touch nurture, and then Rise owns the lead and it changes campaign. Peak answer windows are 8am–noon and after 5pm Mountain — that's where the dial blocks go.

The automatic follow-ups stop the moment Salesforce shows contact, a reply, an appointment or a dead status — which is why the desk logs the outcome of every dial. The automatic text that follows a desk call ("I just tried calling …") fills the gap when a dial goes to voicemail.

Day 1 belongs to the desk

Both Day-1 calls are made by whoever is on the desk the moment the lead lands — the engine rings them with the lead's name and trade. Nothing else on the desk outranks a fresh lead for those two dials.

Days 2–10 come off the task list

Six "Cadence call N of 6 — Day D" tasks are created on every new lead automatically (owner = the lead owner; weekend due dates roll to Monday; closed as "(not needed)" the moment the lead is booked, dead or parked). "My open tasks due today" is the call list. Completing the task with the outcome is the log.

3b · After Day 45 — the lead changes campaign, it doesn't die

Every campaign below is automated text + email + social. No human until the homeowner replies; still no AI calls to anyone who hasn't engaged. Original source stays on the record; each campaign stamps itself as the sub-source (§7).

Aged-lead

Monthly or quarterly by age band — 45–90, 90–180, 180+ days. Seasonal text + email, one touch every 2–4 weeks.

Rehash

Quoted-not-sold or ran-no-sale. A quote-anchored text + email track; a reply goes back to the rep who ran it.

Weather-event

A live hail, high-wind, hard-freeze or snow-load event, raised by a real-time feed → within 24–48 hours the engine filters owned leads by ZIP and storm footprint → a storm-specific text + email series, social on the same schedule.

Promotion · financing · trend

A new offer, a rate change, a crew opening, or a live trend the feed surfaces → the matching segment gets the matching message.

Status: a later phase, after the Day 8–10 and nurture build. Limitation today: the automatic follow-ups and reminders run for vendor-posted leads; a lead the desk types in by hand gets the first text and email and the six cadence tasks, but no automatic follow-ups yet.

4 · On the call

What to say, what to book, what to log.

Call-center best practice, in Christina's voice. She doesn't have to read it — it's the shape of a good call: ask where, lead with their request, offer two times, book, confirm, log. Every script assumes the homeowner already got the text that says "we'll call you shortly." Any set — a call, a text reply with a day, or a time picked online — moves the lead to Appointment Requested; the desk makes it Appointment Set once it's confirmed by phone.

"Hi [first name], it's Christina with Rise Renovation — you just asked for a free [windows] estimate online, so I'm calling right away. If they pause: "It's a free, no-pressure look at the [windows] — about 60 to 90 minutes, and you'll have real numbers before we leave."

Inbound calls from a pay-per-call vendor — the 90-second rule. Vendors like ResultCalls bill for a connected call once it runs past about 90 seconds; a shorter call is creditable. So the first minute of every inbound call is a qualify-or-release minute, in this order, before any selling:
1. Where — "What's the address or ZIP for the project?" (in our service area?)
2. What — "And this is for windows / roofing / a bath?" (a trade we sell? replacement, not repair?)
3. Who — "Are you the homeowner?" (a single-family home — apartments and condos are a dead lead and a credit)
4. How much — "Roughly how many windows?" (meets the minimum: 4 windows, or 2 + a sliding glass door)
Insurance-claim work, storm or hail claims, and anything medical or unrelated fail the test too. Any answer that fails → end warmly inside 90 seconds: "I appreciate the call — that's outside what we do, so I don't want to waste your time. Best of luck with it." Then log Dead NO CREDIT with the reason, same minute. A qualified caller gets the booking script above. Moving fast here isn't rude; it's what keeps bad calls off the invoice.

Non-negotiables on every call

  • Ask the service area first. One question, before the pitch — it saves the homeowner's time and ours.
  • Book on the call. Confirm the address, who needs to be home (all decision-makers), and the project. Windows minimum: 4 windows, or 2 windows plus a sliding glass door (one door counts as two windows).
  • Offer two times, never an open question. "Thursday or Friday?" closes; "when's good?" stalls.
  • Log every attempt the moment it ends — set the Status or the Desk Category, with the outcome and the next step. The automatic texts read those two fields; "Called Today" alone keeps them going. The vendor will not credit a lead we can't show we worked.
  • Set the status honestly: New – Inquiry → No Contact Made → Appointment Requested → Appointment Set → Dead, or Dead NO CREDIT when the lead was bad.
  • Anything odd — wrong trade, out of area, disconnected number, prior customer, "remove me" — note it on the lead and flag it in #leads the same day. That's the credit clock starting. A "remove me" or STOP comes out of every call list immediately.
  • Test leads (TEST, Jack Doe, Jenny Doe) are never called.
5 · Appointments, replies and credits

Protect what we booked. Get paid back for what was bad.

Appointments

  • Confirmation text the same day, then two automatic reminders from the appointment time in Salesforce: the day before ("Reminder from Rise Renovation: your [windows] estimate is [Thursday at 2:00 PM] at [street]. Reply YES to confirm, or reply with a better time if you need to reschedule.") and the morning of ("Rise Renovation — see you today at [2:00 PM] for your [windows] estimate. Please have everyone who'll be part of the decision home."). The Appointment Time field (date and time) is what the reminders and the reports read — set it the moment a time is booked.
  • Self-booked appointments. A homeowner can book their own estimate on Rise's online form — five steps, 10am / 2pm / 6pm Mountain slots, Monday to Saturday. It lands as Appointment Requested with a High-priority task "Confirm self-booked appointment". The desk confirms by phone — the time, every decision-maker home, the address, the project minimum — then sets Appointment Set and assigns the rep.
  • Confirmation Status drives dispatch. A set appointment not confirmed by the day before gets a call. Voice-AI confirmation calls come in October, only for homeowners who have engaged.
  • A no-show is not a dead lead: re-book within 24 hours, two attempts, then hand back to the desk.
  • Every set appointment is reported back to the vendor weekly, automatically (Thursdays) — Lead ID, appointment, demo, sold, net job sold, credits flagged. That report is how we win credits and how the vendor tunes what they send us.

Replies

Every homeowner text lands on the lead in Salesforce as a completed task ("Text reply: …") and in #leads. Either one means the same thing: call them. "Reschedule", "can't make it" and "a different day" are read as a proposed time — call them.

Credits

Form leads: most vendors credit leads that are out of area, duplicates, the wrong trade, or wrong / disconnected numbers — if we ask inside their window (typically 10 days) with the Lead ID and a reason. The desk flags it by setting Dead NO CREDIT with a note; Growth Mindset files the claims as a weekly list and tracks each one to its answer. Repair-only requests and homeowners we simply couldn't reach are ours to keep — that is the cost of the model, and why the cadence matters.

Issue rate = leads marked Dead NO CREDIT ÷ leads delivered (wrong number, out of area, wrong trade, duplicate, no consent). Over 25% for a source is a red line: pause the source and claim the credits (§7).

Pay-per-call: a call that ends inside ~90 seconds is creditable; a qualified call that runs longer is billable whether or not it books. Disqualify fast, log Dead NO CREDIT with the reason, and Growth Mindset files the credit the same week.

Voice AI never does

First contact on a purchased lead, anything before the homeowner has engaged, pricing or scope conversations. Those stay human. Voice agents run on a pinned model version inside Retell, like every other AI in the flow.

Who runs it — Mission Control

One decision-making AI, one rules engine, one reviewer, and fixed automation for every stage — the leads keep moving with every AI switched off. People make every live first call and approve what the machine proposes.

Capacity — the honest math

Per lead, the human work is seven dial attempts (about two minutes each with logging) plus live conversations — roughly 15–20 minutes over ten days. Industry rule: one agent = 20 leads a day = 150 dials. Ten new leads a day is ~60–70 dials and ~2.5 human hours; fifteen a day (~90–105 dials, ~3.5 hours) is the ceiling for two part-time hands; 20–25 a day is a full-time inside-sales seat.

Staffing plan. Now: the desk owns Day 1 and inbound, with a fixed afternoon block for the Day 2–5 calls. Trigger to add a person: five straight weekdays at 15+ new leads/day, or the day volume steps up with the next vendor or a cap increase. The profile (changed Sept 27): a seasoned inside salesperson who knows how to work internet leads, not an entry-level dialer; remote is fine; paid at the top of the market (base + per-sit + close bonus; Nick sets the numbers). No outsourced answering or appointment-setting shops.
7 · How we know it's working

The control system and the numbers.

The control system

  • Cadence tasks, created automatically. Six call tasks with due dates land on every new lead. "My open tasks due today" is the call list; completing the task with an outcome is the log.
  • The desk list view ("Internet Leads – Desk") stays the Day-1 cockpit: new leads, Called Today, Desk Category, Desk Note. The Home page is the desk dashboard (Appendix).
  • The 4:30pm Mountain "Desk check" in #leads, every weekday: today's intake by source · cadence calls still open, by owner · leads with no logged call after 24 hours · sales today and month to date. Non-zero on the open lists two days running gets raised.
  • The vendor disposition report runs every Thursday morning, automatically — the same data as the scorecard, so the reporting is free.
  • The ledger of record is the "Rise Lead Master Ledger" — a Google Sheet in the shared drive that fills itself from Salesforce every hour: leads, contacted, set, sits, sold, cancels, gross and net by day, source and owner, with the red lines below shading red on their own. The Friday brief reads from it.

The sales numbers — gross, installed, net

Every purchased lead is judged through to the money, on the stages Rise already runs in Salesforce. Three words, three meanings: Won = signed at the table · Closed = installed and collected · Lost = no-sale at the demo, or a signed job that cancelled (rescission or later). Net is the one marketing cost is measured against; net sales growth = closed dollars, trailing 30 days vs the 30 before.
