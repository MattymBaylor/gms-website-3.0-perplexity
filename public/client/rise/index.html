1 · The one rule

Be first, then be persistent.

A homeowner who fills out a form is talking to three or four contractors at once. The first one to reach them wins the appointment about 80% of the time. After that first minute, the winner is whoever keeps showing up — six attempts across seven days, on more than one channel — without becoming a pest.

30sVendor-graded standard for first contact. Rise texts and emails in about 6 seconds.
4×Lift in contact rate from calling within the first minute versus the first hour.
80%Of the time, the first contractor to make contact gets the appointment.
50%Of homeowners prefer text or email to a phone call. The text opens the door; the call walks through it.

Three habits that make the rule real

  • Dial the second it lands. The #leads alert and the Salesforce list are the signal. Two calls back to back ("double tap"), then move on.
  • Never skip a day of the cadence. The task list decides what gets called today, not memory.
  • Log it or it didn't happen. Every attempt in Salesforce the moment it ends — that record is also how we win credits from the vendor.

3 · The seven-day cadence

Six calls, three texts, three emails.

The person only owns the calls; the machine owns the rest. Peak answer windows are 8am–noon and after 5pm Mountain — that's where the dial blocks go.

Day 1 belongs to the desk

Both Day-1 calls are made by whoever is on the desk the moment the lead lands. Nothing else on the desk outranks a fresh lead for those two dials.

Salesforce creates the cadence calls as tasks on every lead. "My open tasks due today" is the call list. Completing the task with an outcome is the log.
4 · On the call

What to say, what to book, what to log.

Call-center best practice, in Christina's voice. She doesn't have to read it — it's the shape of a good call: lead with their request, offer two times, book, confirm, log. Every script assumes the homeowner already got the text that says "I'm calling you now."

Live answer — Day 1, the double tap
"Hi [first name], it's Christina with Rise Renovation — you just asked for a free [windows] estimate at [street], so I'm calling right away. I've got a couple of openings this week — is Thursday afternoon or Friday morning better for you?"

If they pause: "It's a free, no-pressure look at the [windows] — about 60 to 90 minutes, and you'll have real numbers before we leave."

"Perfect — Thursday at 2. I've got [street], [city]. Will everyone who'd be part of the decision be home? Great. And it's [4 windows / the front of the house], right? You'll get a text from me in a minute confirming Thursday at 2, and a reminder the day before. Anything you'd like the estimator to know ahead of time?"

Inbound calls from a pay-per-call vendor — the 90-second rule. Vendors like ResultCalls bill for a connected call once it runs past about 90 seconds; a shorter call is creditable. So the first minute of every inbound call is a qualify-or-release minute, in this order, before any selling:
1. Where — "What's the address or ZIP for the project?" (in our service area?)
2. What — "And this is for windows / roofing / a bath?" (a trade we sell?)
3. Who — "Are you the homeowner?"
4. How much — "Roughly how many windows?" (meets the minimum: 4 windows, or 2 + a sliding glass door)
Any answer that fails → end warmly inside 90 seconds: "I appreciate the call — that's outside what we do, so I don't want to waste your time. Best of luck with it." Then log Dead NO CREDIT with the reason, same minute. A qualified caller gets the booking script above. Moving fast here isn't rude; it's what keeps bad calls off the invoice.

Non-negotiables on every call

  • Book on the call. Confirm the address, who needs to be home (all decision-makers), and the project. Windows minimum: 4 windows, or 2 windows plus a sliding glass door (one door counts as two windows).
  • Offer two times, never an open question. "Thursday or Friday?" closes; "when's good?" stalls.
  • Log every attempt the moment it ends — attempt number, outcome, next step. The vendor will not credit a lead we can't show we worked.
  • Set the status honestly: New – Inquiry → No Contact Made → Appointment Requested → Appointment Set → Dead, or Dead NO CREDIT when the lead was bad.
  • Anything odd — wrong trade, out of area, disconnected number, prior customer — note it on the lead and flag it in #leads the same day. That's the credit clock starting.
  • Test leads (TEST, Jack Doe, Jenny Doe) are never called.
5 · Appointments, replies and credits

Protect what we booked. Get paid back for what was bad.

Appointments

  • Confirmation text the same day; reminder the day before and the morning of. Voice-AI confirmation calls follow in October.
  • A no-show is not a dead lead: re-book within 24 hours, two attempts, then hand back to the desk.
  • Every set appointment is reported back to the vendor weekly — Lead ID, appointment, demo, sold, net job sold. That report is how we win credits and how the vendor tunes what they send us.

Replies

Every homeowner text lands on the lead in Salesforce as a completed task ("Text reply: …") and in #leads. Either one means the same thing: call them. Nothing to check, nothing to repost.

Credits

Form leads: most vendors credit leads that are out of area, duplicates, wrong trade, or wrong/disconnected numbers — if we ask inside their window (typically 10 days) with the Lead ID and a reason. The desk flags it by setting Dead NO CREDIT with a note; Growth Mindset files the request. Leads we simply couldn't reach after six attempts are ours to keep — that is the cost of the model, and why the cadence matters.

Pay-per-call: a call that ends inside ~90 seconds is creditable; a qualified call that runs longer is billable whether or not it books. Disqualify fast, log Dead NO CREDIT with the reason, and Growth Mindset files the credit the same week.

7 · How we know it's working

The control system and the numbers.

The control system

  • Cadence tasks, created automatically. Six call tasks with due dates land on every new lead. "My open tasks due today" is the call list; completing the task with an outcome is the log.
  • The desk list view ("Internet Leads – Desk") stays the Day-1 cockpit: new leads, Called Today, Desk Category, Desk Note.
  • Two automatic posts to #leads, 4:30pm MT daily: cadence tasks overdue (lead, day, owner) and leads with no logged attempt in 24 hours. Non-zero two days running gets raised.
  • Weekly scorecard in the Friday brief: contact rate, set rate, attempts per retired lead — per vendor. Same data feeds the vendor's disposition report, so the reporting is free.

What "good" looks like, per vendor, per month

≥60%Contact rate — reached a human
≥30%Appointment-set rate — of contacted
≥80%Set-to-confirmed
6Logged attempts (or a booked appointment) before any lead is retired

Any vendor that misses these for two straight weeks gets reviewed with them, then paused if it doesn't move. Volume never overrides quality.
